# Roadmap — the order of work, and why this order

**Written 2026-08-20, updated 2026-09-05 against engine 3.56.2.** The scope is the full engine: `CAPABILITIES.md` is
the map of what exists and `ARCHITECTURE.md` is the shape it is being built into.

**This supersedes the ordering in `private/PRIORITY.md`**, which was written before the WebGPU
port and ranked work on the assumption that feature parity would not be chased. That refusal is
withdrawn: it rested on a single-package engine where every consumer pays for every feature, and
the package split removes the premise.

**Track P's `§14` was displaced and is now closed.** The design's phase list gave CH-6 to sleeping,
budgets, four LOD tiers with conserving merges and `fingerprintChemistry`. The build re-scoped CH-6
onto something it found first — the temperature reading every ignition criterion has to read was

criteria built on sand — so `§14` was displaced rather than dropped, and stood as the honest gap in
this row for three phases. **CH-10 shipped it on 2026-08-27**, and the fingerprint earned its keep
immediately: it found that a gas cell reported a different temperature depending on whether some
other parcel had happened to realise its chunk first, which put an order dependence into convection
and from there into whether a log ignites on tick N or tick N+1.

**What the row is for.** Every other track adds a capability to the engine; this one adds a
_substrate_. `AGENTS.md` already argues that one wind sampled once is the difference between a
place and a collection of effects, and the same argument reaches further than air: a scene where wet
wood burns as readily as dry wood does not cohere either. The design's own test of whether it is a
model rather than a set of rules is `§8.3` — the two pyrolysis branches have different activation
energies, so fast heating gives volatiles and slow heating gives char, which means a consumer who
buries a log in a low-oxygen pit gets charcoal without anybody having implemented charcoal.

**The row names mechanisms rather than nouns**, which is the property that has held an `XL` in every
track that had it. Two nouns are in it anyway and the design gives both a stop condition: "the
library", which can ship at whatever size the demo needs, and "the atmosphere field", which is the
only part that is not per-object arithmetic and whose fallback is per-parcel local ambient with no
grid at all.

**CH-0 is done, 2026-08-26, and it cost less than the row's shape suggested.** Six modules and six
test files: the element set, the species-by-element matrix, the registry, compositions, the
conservation reduction and the standard table. What it proves is the one thing a foundation phase
can: `elementTotals` exists and is asserted _before_ there is anything to conserve through, so the
day CH-3 first moves matter there is already a test that says whether it moved the right amount.

**Two numbers were wrong before it and are right after**, and both were found by building rather
than by review. The design's own worked substance summed its mass fractions to 1.115, because
moisture had been written as a seventh fraction beside six that already summed to one —
`massFractions` refuses that, naming the sum, on the first substance anybody wrote. And
`AGENTS.md` had said "thirteen engine packages plus the two on the language's own version line"

**CH-1 is done the same day, and it is the phase the rest of the track leans on.** Enthalpy is the
state and temperature is a reading off a curve, so a phase change stopped being an event and became
a flat region — which means nothing in the package branches on melting and nothing ever will. The
acceptance test is literal: a kettle at 373.15 K takes 2,256,400 J per kilogram without its
temperature moving, asserted at 101 points across the plateau.

**Two things came out better than the design guessed.** The curve is **exact at its knots** rather
than approximate, because every species' `cp` is linear in `T` and the trapezoid rule integrates a
linear integrand with no error — so the tests assert to twelve places instead of to a tolerance.
And swapping the working composition at each transition, which was written for bookkeeping, is what
gives ice, water and steam three different slopes from one substance: 2108, 4182 and 1996 J/(kg·K),
each read back out of the finished curve.

**It cost 2,029 bytes gzipped**, 72% on top of CH-0 and the largest proportional step this entry
point will ever take, because it is the second thing in an empty package.

**CH-2 is done, and it is the phase that decides whether a fire reads as a place.** The time for a
heat front to reach a depth goes as `ρ·c·d²/k`, which for oak is six seconds at a millimetre and
four hours at fifty — so a parcel with one temperature is wrong by that whole factor, in the
direction that flashes every log in the world over at once. Shells of equal mass fix it, and the
measured result is the design's claim verbatim: under 20 kW/m² a 2 mm slab's surface passes 300 °C
in 29 seconds and an 80 mm one takes 400, while that log's **core is five hundredths of a kelvin
above the room**.

**The interesting part is that it is sub-linear**, and it is what a single-temperature model gets
backwards. Forty times the thickness costs fourteen times the time, not forty, because a thick slab
is thermally thick: the front never reaches the far side, so the surface stops caring how much wood
is behind it. A lumped parcel is exactly linear in mass and is therefore wrong at both ends rather
than at one.

**The acceptance thresholds were set from measurement, and the first draft of them was wrong in both
directions** — it expected equilibrium after 333 simulated seconds where oak needs five thousand,
and a time ratio of five where the real one is fourteen. Both were corrected by probing the built
mechanism rather than by adjusting it, which is the only order that keeps a test honest.

**CH-3 is done, and the assertion CH-0 was built for finally has something to conserve through.**
Elements hold over 100,000 ticks to a relative drift under 1e-12, and it passed on the first run.
The two refusals that make it work are the element balance at registration — which cannot be a
warning, because an unbalanced reaction invents matter every time it runs and every symptom of that
is subtle — and the proportional clamp, which cuts competing reactions by the same factor rather
than first-come, because first-come makes the answer depend on registration order.

**It found a contradiction in its own design.** `§7` asks for log-spaced knots with `log-k` stored
"so the interpolation is in the variable that is nearly linear", and the paragraph above it bans
transcendentals from the tick — but reading back a stored `log k` needs an `exp` at every lookup.
The resolution is better than either half: knots uniform in `1/T`, where `ln k` is _exactly_ linear
rather than nearly, with `k` stored and interpolated. Measured under 0.5% against the closed form,
which is inside the spread of the activation energies themselves.

**And it left one thing knowingly approximate, as CH-5's gate.** A substance's enthalpy curve is
built from its initial composition, so as reactions run the temperature read back drifts by however
much the true heat capacity has moved — about 20% of the rise for cellulose becoming char. Energy
stays exact, since the joules are added and subtracted directly. Ignition is where temperature
accuracy decides everything, so the fix belongs to the phase that needs it, and it is written down:
one `h(T)` curve per species, a shell's enthalpy the mass-weighted sum, inverted by a search.

**CH-4 is done, and it was the piece this row warned about.** The estimate said the atmosphere was
the only part that is not per-object arithmetic and the largest single unknown, and that held: it
took three corrections the other four phases did not need.

**Transport is flux-form rather than semi-Lagrangian**, which the design chose for stability.
Semi-Lagrangian is not conservative, and conservation is what every phase of this track is asserted
on — so the CFL limit was accepted and handled by the sub-stepping that was already there for
stiffness. **Pressure is `n·T` and not molar density**, which the first attempt got wrong: heating
a cell does not change its moles, so nothing expanded, nothing got lighter, and the plume simply did
not happen. And **the live set is bounded with growth gated on a cell having something to carry** —
without that the `+y` face of every boundary cell reached upward every sweep and the field built a
tower of ordinary air to its cap.

**The acceptance test is the argument for the whole package.** A sealed room runs out of oxygen and
the burning stops, with unburnt fuel left over, and nothing anywhere was told to stop it: the rate
of a reaction with no oxidiser is zero by mass action. **Gas-phase combustion is not a second
solver** either — a cell is handed to the same `reactShell` a shell is.

**Two things the tests got wrong and the model got right**, both worth carrying: a sealed _room_
stratifies, because fuel added on top of a full atmosphere makes its cell denser and it sinks, so
the assertion moved to a single sealed cell; and 0.2 kg of methane in a cubic metre of air reads
15.9% oxygen before anything burns, because dilution is not consumption.

**CH-5 is done, and it is where the parcels and the air stopped being two simulations.** Nothing in
the tick applies a flux any more: a consumer says what is radiating and what is touching what, and
every parcel works out what reaches it. `1/r²` is one line and it is the whole reason "leading a
piece of wood near the fire" has a right answer.

**The phase was split, and the reason is on record.** The map had CH-5 as transport _and_ the nine
ignition and extinction criteria. Those rest on a temperature this model does not yet read
accurately — CH-3 left a substance's enthalpy curve built from its initial composition, so the
reading drifts as reactions run, by about 20% of the rise for cellulose becoming char. A criterion
that fires at 623 K cannot sit on a number that can be a hundred kelvin out, so CH-6 gets the
criteria and opens by fixing the reading.

**Two bugs the conservation assertion found that nothing else would have.** Gas moving between a
parcel and the air was moving **without its enthalpy**, which is an energy leak whose symptom is a
parcel that quietly cools as it vents. And the field's per-face transport caps allowed six faces to
drain more than a cell held, with the non-negativity clamp silently absorbing the difference — mass
created from nothing, at 8.6e-9 and compounding.

**And one that was not a bug at all**, which cost the longest. The drift sat in nitrogen, which
nothing in the scene reacts. `elementTotals` adds live chunks to the far-field ledger, and the
ledger books a new chunk's air as having come from outside — so nitrogen is two terms of about
16,800 moles cancelling to nearly nothing, and a measure relative to the _net_ divides by one and
reports the last bits of a cancellation as a leak. Measured against the magnitudes involved it is
1e-11, which is where double precision lives. The test now says so, and says why.

**CH-6 opened by reversing a decision the design made and CH-1 shipped, and the reversal deleted
more than it added.** `§3`'s per-substance enthalpy curve was built from a substance's _initial_
composition, so the temperature it reported drifted as reactions changed what was actually there.
No ignition criterion could be allowed to read that, which is why the criteria waited for it.

**The repair is smaller than the thing it repairs, because the latent heat was never sensible
enthalpy's job.** It is already in the formation enthalpies CH-0 stores: Hess's law gives 333,611
J/kg for ice melting against a literature 333,550, and 2,442,631 for water boiling — which is the
value at 298.15 K rather than the 2,256,400 quoted at the boiling point, and that is the row that
settles it. The latent heat at any temperature stops being stored and becomes a consequence of the
heat capacities either side.

So a phase change became **a reaction with a temperature gate**, its plateau emerging from the
substep-and-clamp solver, and what was left was purely sensible heat — a quadratic, inverted in
closed form with **one square root**. The package got **231 bytes smaller**, the first time this
entry point has gone down.

**Two things the build corrected.** `Instant` kinetics would let the clamp boil a whole kilogram in
one sub-step, absorbing two and a half megajoules and throwing the temperature hundreds of kelvin
the wrong way — a phase change needs a finite gain through an opening gate, which is a proportional
controller and settles just above the transition by an amount proportional to the flux arriving.
And the solver's stiffness estimate needed an **enthalpy term**: reactant availability alone called
a step that converted a tenth of the ice well-resolved, while it undershot by sixteen kelvin.

**And the criteria followed it.** Ignition is five conditions, so `ignite` supplies a pilot and
**holding a match to wet wood does nothing** — which is the API telling the truth about what a match
is, and is the assertion the whole machinery exists to make possible. Extinction is four, and a
smoulder survives air a flame cannot, which is why smothering leaves embers.

**Two resolution errors, both found by things failing to catch that should have.** A charring
reaction that produced only carbon and steam gave off nothing combustible, so the mixture above the
surface never entered its limits — the criterion was right and the test's fuel was wrong. And the
flammability check was reading a **cell average** where a flame sits in a boundary layer: a log at
1,063 K pouring off nearly a kilogram per square metre per second registered 1.7% combustible gas in
the cubic metre around it and never caught. The mixture is now read at the surface, as a ratio of
what is leaving it to what is sweeping past — which also thins in a wind, so the same arithmetic
that blows a flame off makes one harder to start.

**CH-6 is complete, and `§21`'s log is asserted end to end.** Seasoned oak in front of a campfire
dries with its surface pinned at the boiling point and its core still at room temperature, then
smokes, then catches — and **a green log at the same fire does not**.

**That counterfactual took three corrections, and every one was the model being right.** The green
log caught at first because steam was counting as fuel: `§21` says a wet surface dilutes the mixture
above it, and the flux had to be split into what is leaving and what of it can burn. It caught again
because the fire was ten times too bright — a square metre of flame at 400 mm is 210 kW/m² against
the 20 the design describes, and with enough heat anything dries and burns. And it caught a third
time when the log was halved to make the test faster, because a thinner wet log has less water per
unit of arriving flux. The thickness is load-bearing.

**The clock is measured rather than asserted from the design.** `§21` wrote 260 s to ignition; this
geometry gives about 950. The tolerance widened and the measurement is written down, per this
track's own stop condition — every number in the run is a literature activation energy, a formation
enthalpy or a measured conductivity, and the one thing that would close the gap is a geometry the
design never pinned down.

**More than half of it needs no renderer, no GPU and no host.** CH-0 through CH-3 are arithmetic
over typed arrays against analytic references, which is the property that made Track B's solver
testable and is deliberate here.

**Track O came in at `L` and the estimate held**, and the reason is the property Track N's
row already records. Its row named mechanisms — "provider-neutral sessions", "typed tools",
"a deterministic test provider" — rather than nouns, and the one decision that could have
blown it was made in the design rather than during the build: what sits in the buffer. A
buffer of concrete actions would have needed staleness machinery per action; a standing
policy alone would have made the model's influence too coarse to be worth the bill. The
floor-plus-one-slot answer made preemption, budget exhaustion and guard failure each a
fallback to something already running rather than three mechanisms of their own.

**What was not in the plan and had to be built anyway**: a preemption entry in the command
log. The floor recomputes on replay, but an observation is _external input_ — the same class
of thing as a keypress — so a replay reran the floor straight past every interruption. The
plan's "replay is exact across a preemption" test is what found it.

**M, N and O were added 2026-08-25**, from a design that proposed them and left them unadded
because editing this file is a commitment rather than a description. N is the one in progress.

**Track M is work this repository previously said nothing waited on.** Row 1.1 above still parks an
entity model in a sentence — "none of which any track waits on" — and that was true when it was

**Cross-module imports and record subtyping are not a track**, and are recorded in N's row rather
than given one. They are a language change asked for while N was in flight, and designed before they were built.

**N's gate is A rather than A, B, F and M**, which is the cheaper sequencing §51 records and the
owner took. Eleven capability surfaces link against the engine as it stands — the loop, time, RNG,
events, persistence, scene, kinematic physics, input, cameras, the whole of audio, and animation

pay the price the original sequencing was avoiding. **The phases whose providers do not exist are
refused in writing rather than mocked**: DS-8 waits on J, and DS-7's navigation and behaviour

**Two of the four ended on 2026-08-26, and the mechanism is what ended them rather than a memory.**
DS-5 and DS-6 waited on M; M shipped, `drift/ecs` and `drift/prefab` were bound, and the sentinels
guarding them failed the suite until every document moved in the same commit. The paragraph that

**It landed the same day.** The five forms are built, and were designed before they were.
What the design decided that §16 and §17 could not, because they were written before the engine
underneath them existed: the language owns a component's shape and asserts a host's; a query loop
opens a pooled cursor through an ordinary capability and hoists a live view, so a field access is a
property load and an array index rather than a host call per field; reads and writes are inferred
the way effects already are, and the declaration is an assertion checked against them; `@editor` is
validated and emitted with `mode` stripping it from a shipping build; and a query runs against the
world in scope, which a system has and a function may declare.

**Measured at 30.4 ns per entity against 28.7 for the same loop written by hand and 388 for a host
call per field** — §13a of the design, which is what the mechanism was chosen for.

**What is left of Track N is what was refused in writing**: DS-7's navigation and behaviour thirds,
which wait on no track at all, and DS-8, which waits on J.

**Track N came in at `XL` and the estimate held.** Fifty-five commits over two days, 14,889 lines of
source across four packages and 8,817 of tests — 687 of them, in 45 files. Its row named mechanisms
rather than nouns, which is the property Track A's row had and Track G's did not, and it is why the
number survived: "capability registry" was the single noun §51 warned about, and it turned out to

**What that says about `M`.** Its row names both — "entities with generational identity" and
"component storage" are mechanisms, "prefabs" and "scene serialization" are nouns. The two nouns
are where its `XL` will be decided, and neither has a design yet.

**Track M came in under its `XL`, and the reason is the one Track N's row already records.** Both
nouns the row below warns about turned out small: a prefab is a value because a live link is Track
K's, and scene serialization is field ids because Track N's Phase 5 had already built them and
proved them across a hot reload. The expensive part of each had been decided somewhere else, which
is what happened to "capability registry" too.

**What it cost instead was the mechanisms** — identity, storage and the scheduler — and two of the
three landed differently from the design. The schedule's ordering was specified as a topological
sort over the _conflict_ graph, which cannot work because a conflict is symmetric; ordering comes
from explicit `after` edges and conflicts give parallel grouping. And the sparse-set trade-off was
measured rather than assumed: a three-component query over 100,000 entities is 4.8× the cost of
walking adjacent data, which is larger than the design guessed and still does not reverse it,
because the thing archetypes lose is the marker toggled per frame.

**Two of these estimates hide a variable, and the warning below about B and K applies to both.**
`N` contains "capability registry" and `M` contains "scene serialization". Track G was estimated
`S` and shipped as the largest track so far because two of its items were single nouns that hid a
decision, and those two are exactly that kind of noun. Track A held its `XL` because its row named
mechanisms rather than nouns; these rows name both.

**Track B came in at `XL` and the estimate held, over six plans in one session.** What is worth
carrying to the tracks that follow is _where the time went_, because it was not where the row said.

**The row's own items were the cheap half.** Substepped soft constraints are a well-documented
formulation; narrowphase is SAT over axes the shape already enumerated; islands are union-find. The
two items the estimate warned about — "vehicles" and "cloth", the single nouns — were decided in the
design and cost one plan between them.

**What it actually cost was the things the row could not name.** A determinism claim that was a
comment and was false, and the gate that makes it true. A `ConvexShape` that could not produce a
contact manifold, because it kept deduped axis _directions_ and discarded the face incidence. An
ordering error in the substep loop that collapsed a stack of ten to the height of one while a single
box rested perfectly. A `shapecast` answering the opposite normal convention from `raycast`, which
made a character read the floor as a wall. And seventy-nine names missing from core's barrel, which
nothing could fail on because a name that is not re-exported is simply a name nobody can import.

**The sentinels split four to two, and the pattern is legible.** `rigid-body-dynamics`,
`joints-constraints`, `world-raycast`, `vehicles` and `cloth` fired; `triggers` and `ragdolls` did
not, because both named a _function_ the implementation turned out not to have. A sentinel naming a
plausible class survives the guess; one naming a function is a guess at a name made before the code
exists. `CAPABILITIES.md` carries that.

**Two features were built, measured, and removed or bounded rather than kept on faith**: ground snap
made the foot gap _worse_ (0.490 metres against 0.457), and distance-based cloth bending resists
bowing but not orientation, so it will not hold a cantilever out however stiff. Both are recorded
where the code was, with the instrument that would justify revisiting them.

**Track J was untouched by the replay primitives added in 2.11.0, and shipping it proved that
right.** `TickTrace` and `fingerprintColliders` are the deterministic-replay half — comparing a
recorded run against a live one, offline, with no transport anywhere. Track J was the _live_ half.
When it landed on 2026-09-03 it borrowed the _shape_ of both and neither line of code: `Fingerprint`
is `fingerprintColliders`' two FNV-1a streams over a different subject, and `InputLog` borrows
`CommandLog`'s tick indexing and shares nothing else. The two halves shared the determinism contract
and nothing else, exactly as this paragraph said, and neither brought the other closer.

**What it did borrow was a decision nobody made for it.** DriftScript's fixed clock is a _step
count_ rather than seconds, chosen in DS-3 for a reason about replay, and that is the only reason a
task's deadline survives a rewind — an absolute step number means the same thing after the clock
moves in either direction. `clocks.ts` had doubted its own shape in writing and deferred the answer
to whoever had a rollback loop; the answer was that the shape was already right, five phases early.

**Track J came in at `XL` against a row that said `L`.** The row omitted the loop seam, the RNG's
state and the language phase that waited on it, and named two networking models where the estimate
had priced one. The separate register of
unpriced work had it at XL and was right.

**Track C's clustered-lights row shipped in 2.2.0, 2026-08-24**, ahead of the image-based-lighting piece
below and for a reason that is about 1.5 rather than about lighting: the compute seam needed a consumer,
because `AGENTS.md` says a feature nothing turns on is written rather than ported, and clustered binning is
the consumer the gate itself names first. The conformance script that compares the two binners is there because of the 2026-08-17 rule about
two implementations of one decision, and it earned its keep twice over: it found two bugs in the CPU
binner that would have shipped as lights missing on WebGL2 alone.

**Track C's first piece has a design, 2026-08-23.** Written because **four separate look controls are standing in for image-based lighting**, three of them added within two days of each other and each carrying an apology at its own definition. Three pieces, in the order they pay off: spherical-harmonic irradiance, a GGX prefilter to replace the box-filtered chain, and a loader for an environment the scene did not capture. The HDR probe and the chain they build on already ship. The acceptance test is literal rather than rhetorical: every one of those four controls goes to 1.

**G, H and J gate on nothing** and run beside Phase 1, so visible progress continues while 1.2 does
the dangerous work.

**Track C is complete, 2026-08-25, and its row was read correctly.** Sized `L` and it was an `L`:
clustered lighting and spherical-harmonic irradiance had already shipped, and the remaining five
pieces — a GGX prefilter, an environment loader, spot lights, IES photometry and area lights — went
in one session. The estimate held where Track G's did not, and the difference is instructive: this
row's items were named as _mechanisms_ rather than as single nouns, and the one that was a bare
noun, "area/LTC", is the one that turned out to hide a decision.

**Extended 2026-09-03, and the row stays complete rather than reopening.** Two rows the costed
census had carried open the whole time were Track C's tail, and this is what happened to them.

- **Environment probes on a grid shipped**, in 3.49.0. The row's own risk was that it "changes the
  pixels of every published scene", so it carried a stop condition written before the work: a grid
  of one had to reproduce the single baked probe this engine already had. It does, measured on one
  GPU against `main` through `ibl-check.mjs`, within about one level of 255 on every figure and with
  the no-probe control identical. What made it affordable was a fold rather than an allocation: the
  probe's `samplerCube` became an octahedral array at the same binding, carrying the reflection and
  the diffuse ambient in one chain, so a grid of any size costs no texture unit and WebGL2's last
  guaranteed one is still free.
- **Fitted LTC was attempted and stopped**, at its own bound and on the same day. The design gave it
  2% of a brute-force integral, 5% at the grazing extreme; the fit holds a few per cent at high
  roughness and misses by two orders of magnitude at low roughness and grazing angles, which is the
  one case a representative point already handles worst and the only reason to reach for an LTC at
  all. `scripts/ltcFit.mjs --check` reproduces it, and the fallback the design wrote — an embedded
  table on the free texture unit — does not apply, because the table is what fails. The sentinel
  stays and `IMPROVEMENTS.md` carries the three things worth trying next.
- **Reopened 2026-09-16 by Wave 4A's task 8, and closed again without building anything.** The
  refusal of the _fit_ holds and is reproducible today: `node scripts/ltcFit.mjs --check` reports
  **172.9% worst, 78.4% over cells carrying more than a twentieth of the surface's albedo**,
  against the design's 2%. What the plan did not know is that **the transform it asked for already
  ships** — `quadCoverage` in `shaders/flat/main.ts` is a linearly transformed cosine with an
  analytic matrix on both backends, at 40.1% and 79.0% worst visible error against 287% for the
  representative point it replaced, and `CAPABILITIES.md` has said so since it landed. A second
  implementation in `packages/core/src/render/gi/` was written, tested against a brute-force
  integral and **withdrawn**: `scripts/lobeMath.mjs` and `scripts/areaSpecular.mjs` are already the
  TypeScript twin of that shader term, and a third copy of one decision is the drift this
  repository keeps paying for rather than a guard against it.

**The row reads as what happened rather than only as what is true**, which is the shape Track K's
row took for the same reason: a track that stopped, was asked for again, and recorded both.

**Two of its items were built differently from the design and both reversals were measured.**

- **Area lights occlude as of 2026-08-28, and the row that said they could not is closed.** The
  reason it gave was that the shadow pool is octahedral and centred on a point and a rectangle is
  not one. A rectangle does have a point — its centre — and the only thing separating an area shadow
  from a point one turned out to be where the filter's width comes from: `areaShadow()` opens an
  ellipse the size of the emitter's projected outline, so the bake, the pooling, the staleness and
  the arrival ramp are the point light's own code on both backends. Measured on
  `pointshadow.html?area=1`: an edge 0.178 m wide along a three-metre strip against 0.044 m across
  it, the softening following the strip when it is turned, and 122 pixels of 1,689,600 between the
  two backends. Off unless a rectangle asks for it, because a scene that already declared one was
  authored against a light that passed through stone.
- **Area lights are not LTC.** A fitted linearly transformed cosine needs 64×64 tables that come
  out of an offline optimisation; what shipped is the exact closed-form polygon integral for the
  diffuse half — which is what LTC reduces to at the identity, so that half is not approximated at
  all — and a representative point for the specular. `CAPABILITIES.md` says which half is which
  rather than letting "area lights" imply the whole of it.
- **They are a branch on a count, not the permutation the design specified.** The spec said a
  permutation because two LUT samplers and a polygon integration is the shape that earns one. There
  are no LUT samplers, and the term compiled in unconditionally costs 44,984 gzipped bytes against
  the 196,910 a doubled corpus costs — which every consumer pays either way. Same reversal
  clustered lighting made, on the same kind of number.

**What the row cost, in bytes, is worth carrying to Tracks D and F**, which also land in the lit
pass: 6,215 gzipped for thirty lines of shared shader, 10,517 for fifty, 44,984 for ninety. It
tracks generated _operands_ rather than authored lines, so a term full of inverse cosines is far
dearer than one full of branches, and `scripts/size-gate.test.mjs` carries the table.

**A defect the row uncovered is worth more than any of its features.** `demo/dev/pointshadow.html`
was written to reproduce a reported bug and is the first page in this repository with a moving
caster under a point light — every point-lit demo here passes `NO_MOVING_CASTERS`. It found that
`PointShadowSystem.advance` never reached the live pair, so a moving caster had **never** been
shadowed by a point light, on either backend, in the whole life of the engine. No published scene
could have caught it.

**Track A is complete, 2026-08-25, and the `XL` was right.** Every item in the row shipped, and
the row is importable end to end: a rigged, morphed model goes glTF → `bake` → `.drft` → stream →
GPU, on both backends. Two container minor versions came out of it — 1.6 for `NODE`, `SKIN` and
`ANIM`, 1.7 for `MORP` — and `NODE` had been _specified_ in `FORMAT.md` §4.3 since v1 with no
writer and no reader, so rigid TRS is what finally built a chunk the format had claimed for its
whole life.

**And it was not, quite: root motion closed on 2026-08-27**, two days after the row was called
complete. It was left out because it is a decomposition of a track rather than a capability the
importer had to carry, and the row's own sentinels said so — `root-motion` and
`skinned-shadow-casting` were both added _as_ Track A closed, precisely to name what the row left.
Both have now closed. **A track called complete over a row that still holds sentinels is a track
that is complete except for what its sentinels name**, which is a sentence worth having in this
document rather than in nobody's head.

**Two decisions were reversed against the superseded plan, both by measurement, and both are the
same shape as Track C's.**

- **The skinning branch is a shader permutation, not a uniform gate.** `ARCHITECTURE.md` §1's rule
  about 2^n is a fact about the _fragment_ corpus: the vertex shader had no permutation axis at
  all, so the first flag cost **1,118 gzipped bytes** and the second **1,674**, against the
  **246,925** one more fragment flag costs. Both together are about 1% of it. A uniform gate would
  have charged every vertex of every static mesh in the engine a branch it can never take.
- **The joint palette is a vertex data texture, not a `uniform mat4[]`.** The plan ruled a texture
  out by citing the 2026-08-07 rule, which governs implicit derivatives in a fragment stage — a
  vertex stage computes none. What the uniform array would have cost is the reason to care:
  sixty joints at the WebGL2 floor, under a humanoid with fingers, on exactly the hardware the
  60 fps gate names. The same texture then carried morph deltas, which is what made that item
  affordable rather than a redesign.

**What the row cost in payload**, and it is worth carrying to Tracks B and K: the animation package
is **5,779 gzipped bytes** over core — a skeleton, sampler, blending, blend trees, a state machine,
IK and retargeting — because everything in it is arithmetic over typed arrays with no GPU code.
Core grew **5,342** for the half that could not be a package, since `RendererApi` is a surface a
package cannot extend. And `@driftengine/drft` grew **38%**, from 3,293 to 4,535, entirely in four
chunk codecs — which is what buys a rig surviving a bake.

**Three estimates in this row came in low and one guard was too narrow, all found the same way.**
Both permutation costs were simulated before building and under-read by 3–7×, because splicing
hand-written WGSL into a duplicated variant does not model what naga emits. And `flatPass`'s
assertion that the vertex variants share a uniform block compared only two of them — equal, because
skinning added a sampler and no uniform — so it would have stayed silent through exactly the change
it was written to catch. Simulate for an order of magnitude; regenerate for a cost; and assert a
property over every variant rather than over the pair that happens to exist.

**Track G was estimated `S` and shipped as the largest single track so far**, which is worth
recording because the estimate was not wrong about the row — it was wrong about the depth two of
its five items would be taken to. "Buses" was read as a flat table of named stages and built as a
full submix tree with nesting, inserts and mix snapshots; and rather than adding the tree beside
`AudioGraph`, the mix was re-founded on it. Neither was in the row's words and both were deliberate
choices taken with the cost stated. **The lesson is that a size column measures a row's items and
not the shape a decision may give them**, so a track whose items are single nouns — "buses",
"terrain", "sessions" — is an estimate with a hidden variable in it.

What made the rewrite survivable is worth carrying to any track that touches something load-bearing:
an offline render of the old mix was frozen **before any source moved**, and the new one had to come
back bit-for-bit identical to it. `scripts/audio-baseline.mjs` is that gate and it is the audio
equivalent of the zero-pixel rule.

Track A and Track E were each planned in full before being restarted, and each superseded an
earlier plan of the same subject: those predated the package split and the second backend, so every
file path in them was wrong.

The animation rewrite reverses two of its predecessor's decisions on measurements rather than on
argument, and both are worth knowing before the row is planned again. The skinning branch is a
**permutation and not a uniform gate** — the vertex shader has no permutation axis at all, so the
2^n cost that §1 of `ARCHITECTURE.md` is written about does not apply to it: a second vertex
variant costs **1,118 bytes gzipped**, measured after generation, against **246,925** for one more
fragment flag — 0.45% of the thing the rule exists to prevent. And the joint palette
is a **vertex data texture and not a uniform array** — the 2026-08-07 rule the old plan cited to
rule a texture out governs implicit derivatives in a fragment stage, which a vertex stage does not
compute, while the uniform array it chose instead caps a rig at sixty joints on the WebGL2 floor.

## Phase 3 — Integration showcase, and it is the whole of what is left

One scene proving the tracks compose: an imported PBR character, skinned and animated, ragdolling
on impact, in a clustered-lit interior with a splat backdrop, a 2D interface over it, spatially
mixed audio, streamed progressively, on both backends, opened in the editor.

**Sixteen tracks are complete and this has not started**, which is the single most important line
in this document. Every track shipped with a demo scene of its own and each proves one thing in
isolation; nothing yet proves two of them in the same frame, and the defects that only appear when
two subsystems share a frame are the ones no unit test has ever caught here.

**And there is a second reason it matters, which is not about defects.** The published scenes ship
no image assets — that is deliberate and it is the payload argument — so **normal maps, ORM maps,
image-based lighting and the metal split are not visible in anything a reader can open**. They are
verified, on hardware, by their own before-and-after campaigns in `shots/`, and by nothing a
visitor sees. The largest material change this engine has ever made is currently invisible to
everyone outside this repository.

### What would have falsified the programme, written before it started

A claim that cannot be disproved is not a claim. These were fixed in advance so that they could not
be moved afterwards, and none of them fired:

| If this had happened                                                                      | It would have meant                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The GPU-driven pipeline could not beat the forward path's frame time on a scene both draw | The rendering ceiling is bounded by WebGPU rather than by effort |
| A new subsystem forced a clock or an unseeded random onto the simulation path             | The lead and the breadth are in conflict, and breadth must yield |
| `core-only` grew to pay for a feature its importer never enabled                          | Modularity was the real asset and the programme was spending it  |
| The editor could not be driven by a person who did not write it                           | The tooling claim is a claim about a demo                        |
| The native host could not reproduce the browser's pixels                                  | The platform seam was held by review rather than by design       |

**The first one deserves its result rather than a tick.** The GPU-driven pipeline's _device_ time is
about twice the forward path's at every radius measured; what it buys is on the CPU, where the
forward path's median grows with its draws from 1.0 to 13.6 ms while the port's stays between 0.7
and 1.7. At the widest radius that is 10.3 ms against 1.5. **Neither holds sixty at the ninety-fifth
percentile past radius 20, and the frame that breaks it is the same on both** — a chunk being
built. At a walking pace the ceiling is the world's generation rather than either renderer. That is
the result, and it is in `docs/CAPABILITIES.md` in those words rather than softened.

### Refusals this phase upheld rather than reversed

**Screen-space global illumination is still refused.** What shipped is world-space: a bounded
distance field with a probe volume behind it, using the screen only as an accelerator. The
requirement is met and the refused technique is still refused, and that distinction is the whole
reason the work was legitimate.

**A navigation mesh was not a refusal and steering still is.** The graph's own source said a mesh
was the row that stays open if a world's walkable space is genuinely a region; a streamed open world
is that world, so the trigger the refusal named fired. **How a character moves along a path remains
a game's feel**, and the line did not move.

**Spherical harmonics past degree 1 for splats reopens conditionally and not before** a
re-measurement. If the re-measurement does not move it, the refusal stands.

### Not in scope, at the programme level

- **Training code in any shipped package.** Weights ship; trainers do not.
- **A second engine in a native language.** The native host runs _this_ engine without a browser.
- **Benchmarks against other engines inside a track.** A track's gate is a fact about this
  repository; statements about other engines belong in the deferred proof suite or nowhere.

### Wave 7, deferred — and the part of it worth keeping whether or not it runs

A comparative proof suite against other browser engines. It is deferred because standing up other
engines to benchmark against is a scope decision somebody takes deliberately rather than a coding
task somebody picks up. **Its rules are worth more than its results and are recorded here so that
anything comparative built later inherits them:**

- **Fairness is the product.** A harness that flatters DriftEngine is worth less than no harness.
- **Every number carries its machine, its browser, its date and its scene.** A number without them
  is not a measurement.
- **Where an engine cannot do something, the report says "does not attempt", never zero.** Scoring
  an absence as a failure is the commonest dishonesty in comparisons like this.
- **The adapters are published with the results.** An unreproducible benchmark is a claim.
- **A similarity reference is generated from the definition, never from any one engine's output** —
  using one engine's frame as the reference scores every other engine on how well it imitates it.
- **If DriftEngine loses a row, that row is the most valuable output of the exercise.**

### Asked for, and deliberately not in 4.0.0

- **A colour grade and a vignette in the composite.** The composite has an output transform, an
  exposure and bloom, and nothing else. A grade belongs there, on both backends, after bloom and
  before the curve.
- **The frame's own reflections on the second pipeline.** It reflects a baked probe or a two-colour
  gradient, so a wet street cannot give back the street lights above it.
- **Frame generation inside the frame**, and the learned reconstruction tier beside it.

## On estimates

Sizes rather than dates, because this repository's velocity is not comparable to a normal team's.
**B and K are the two most likely to be underestimated by a wide margin**: a constraint solver and
an editor are both multi-year specialities elsewhere.

**K came in under, and the reason is what it decided not to be.** An editor is a multi-year
speciality when it is a _product_ — a window, a docking layout, a property drawer per type, an undo
stack, an asset browser. This one is the model underneath one: a tree flatten, a schema walk, a
snapshot round trip and a `UiNode` builder, at 10.3 KB. The pixels belong to a consumer and the
"not in scope" list above already said so about a graph editor. That is a scope decision rather than
a velocity result, and it is the honest reading of why the estimate was wide.

**The scope decision was then taken the other way, and that does not change the reading.** A graph
editor exists as of 2026-09-15, outside `packages/`; Track K's 10.3 KB is unchanged by it, which is
exactly the point this paragraph was making. An editor is a multi-year speciality when it is a
product. This repository has now built both, and they are different artefacts in different
directories.
